eps = e/w = 2.71828/1024 = 2.6546e-3 ; eps*N with N = n_records.
delta = e^-d = e^-3 = 4.979e-2  -> at most 4.979% of keys may exceed eps*N.

CAVEAT (both systems, not a difference between them):
  The classical Count-Min eps/delta guarantee is over the RANDOM CHOICE OF
  HASH FUNCTIONS, and requires the hashes to be independent of the input.
  Neither system has that. zk-Analytics fixes CM_SEEDS as three public
  compile-time constants (aggregator/core/src/lib.rs:40); zkSketch fixes a
  public Poseidon2 instance (sketch/countmin_final.rs:147). When the curator
  is also the prover, it knows the addressing before choosing what to
  ingest, so a chosen-key adversary can drive the estimate far past eps*N
  in EITHER system and the proof still verifies -- a proof of correct
  sketching is not a proof of accurate sketching. The numbers above are
  therefore an AVERAGE-CASE law measured by re-drawing the key set per
  seed, not a worst-case bound. Poseidon2 makes finding such keys
  harder to search for than splitmix64, but NOT by a security margin at
  this geometry: driving one key's estimate up needs a collision in all
  d=3 rows, i.e. ~w^d = 2^30 offline trial keys either way. For them each
  trial is one splitmix64 finalizer (~ns) and their key encoding
  (FNV-1a, lib.rs:342) is itself invertible, so a single row is matched in
  closed form; for us each trial is a Poseidon2 sponge (~us). That is a
  ~10^3 work factor, not a cryptographic separation. Raising d or w is the
  only real defence, and it is equally available to both systems.

NOT REPORTED BY CONSTRUCTION: per-key agreement between the two estimates.
  The two systems use different hash families over different key encodings
  (their 15-byte key_id -> FNV-1a -> splitmix64 -> mod 1024 vs our u64 ->
  Poseidon2 sponge -> digest[row] mod w), so their bucket assignments are
  unrelated. Only the ERROR LAWS are comparable.
