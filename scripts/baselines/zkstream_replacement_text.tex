% ---------- R1: replaces zk1.tex:153--154 ----------
Although ZKPs have been increasingly explored for verifiable analytics, most
existing systems focus on computations over static datasets. The few ZKP-based
works that consider streaming data prove each released result over a bounded,
explicitly enumerated portion of the stream: a selected sub-stream or a sliding
window of recent records~\cite{FioreTuckerSignedStreams2022}, or a fixed-size
message array supplied to one operator invocation~\cite{zkStreamMiddleware2024}.
This differs from the conventional streaming model, in which query results are
continuously released over all data observed up to the current time.
To the best of our knowledge, no prior non-interactive ZKP system instantiates
and evaluates a summary maintained over the whole observed prefix.  We do not
rest that claim on a reading of the literature alone: Section~\ref{sec:rq-zkstream}
ports the stateful-operator mechanism that zkStream itself
proposes (its Section~4.4) to a Count-Min update and measures it on our hardware.

% ---------- R2: replaces the zkStream clause at zk1.tex:222 ----------
zkStream~\cite{zkStreamMiddleware2024} compiles each operator of a stream program
to a separate ZoKrates/Groth16 circuit over a compile-time-fixed message array,
and passes Poseidon-committed values between adjacent operator proofs.  Its
Section~4.4 further proposes stateful operators, ``passing the state as a
confidential value from one to the next invocation of the operator'', and its
artifact ships the commitment machinery that would realize this.  The shipped
benchmarks, however, never instantiate it: across the artifact's operator
circuits each is either a commitment \emph{producer} or a commitment
\emph{consumer}, never both, so no proof carries an evolving object from
$\com(S_{i-1})$ to $\com(S_i)$, and no benchmarked operator maintains a summary of
the accumulated stream.

% ---------- R3: replaces zk1.tex:383--387 ----------
This mirrors the proof structure of the prior stream-ZKP
systems SPHinx~\cite{FioreTuckerSignedStreams2022} and
zkStream~\cite{zkStreamMiddleware2024}, which emit a separate proof for each
released computation and link adjacent proofs through committed values.  The
difference is what crosses the link: there, a committed \emph{result} of a
bounded computation; here, the committed \emph{state} $\com(S_i)$ of a summary
that every later batch continues to update.

% ---------- R4: replaces zk1.tex:1194--1200 (the Baselines head) ----------
\textbf{Baselines.}  \emph{External.}  zkStream~\cite{zkStreamMiddleware2024} is
the closest deployed system and we compare against it head to head
(Section~\ref{sec:rq-zkstream}).  Its Section~4.4 explicitly proposes carrying
operator state forward as a confidential value, and its artifact ships the
Poseidon commit-in/commit-out mechanism: one circuit emits a committed
per-slice result, another consumes a fixed number of such commitments as public
inputs.  What the artifact does not contain is a circuit that is both, i.e.\ an
update proof taking $\com(S_{i-1})$ and returning $\com(S_i)$ over the same
evolving object, and no shipped benchmark maintains a sketch.  We therefore do
not claim that zkStream cannot be extended; we extend it ourselves, in its own
toolchain, and report the cost.  SPHinx~\cite{FioreTuckerSignedStreams2022} is
not portable in the same way: a SPHinx proof covers one statistic over one
queried window with verification linear in that window's length, so no choice of
input size aligns the two statements.  We additionally compare against
zk-Analytics~\cite{XXX} at matched semantics.

% ---------- R5: replaces zk1.tex:1206--1213 (RETRACTION of the stand-in) ----------
\emph{Composition:} the per-batch proof chain against the proof forest across
machine counts.  The chain is an ablation of \sys\ against itself: it holds the
leaf circuit, the backend, and the field fixed and varies only the composition
topology, so it isolates what recursive composition buys.  We do not offer it as
a proxy for SPHinx or zkStream; the external comparison is carried by
Section~\ref{sec:rq-zkstream} and by zk-Analytics.

% ---------- R6: positioning sentence to OPEN the new zkStream section ----------
This section compares \sys's \emph{update circuit} against a direct port of
zkStream to the same Count-Min geometry on the same machine.  It deliberately
does not compare composition topologies.  ZoKrates 0.8.8 ships exactly one
recursion gadget, a BW6-761 verifier for BLS12-377 Groth16 proofs
(\texttt{stdlib/snark/gm17.zok}); this is a two-chain, not a cycle, so it admits
exactly one level of recursion and no logarithmic-depth composition, and
zkStream never invokes it.  Any ZoKrates realization of a streaming sketch is
therefore a chain by construction.  The proof forest is consequently evaluated
against \sys's own chain (Section~\ref{sec:rq-topology}), where its benefit is
audit compactness and multi-machine makespan rather than single-machine prover
time.
