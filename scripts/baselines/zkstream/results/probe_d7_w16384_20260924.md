# Compile probe: d=7, w=2^14 (the paper's default geometry), 2026-09-24

Purpose: find out whether the ZoKrates Count-Min operator (our circuit, following
zkStream's stateful-operator design) compiles at the paper's default geometry. This
settles plan item W4(c) / E-19 with a measurement.

Command (memory-capped scope, 60-minute timeout):

    timeout 60m systemd-run --user --scope -q -p MemoryMax=300G \
      ./run_zkstream.py --d 7 --w 16384 --k 2 --chunks 2 --prune

Outcome: **did not compile within 60 minutes** at k=2 records per chunk.

- The step that did not finish was `zokrates compile` of `cm_op.zok`. It ran single-threaded
  at 99.9% CPU.
- Resident memory was 76.4 GB when sampled at 38 min, and still growing.
- The timeout killed the job at 60 min. No CSV row was written, because the driver
  only records a step after it completes.
- For comparison, the earlier one-record probe (packed8 probe, 2026-09-13) compiled
  in 146.8 s at 16.2 GB. Going from 1 record to 2 records therefore grows compile
  time and memory far more than linearly.
- The k=4 probe was cancelled, since it cannot do better than k=2.
- Machine: Threadripper PRO 9975WX, 32 cores, 559 GiB. The box was shared, with load
  of about 9-18 during the run.

Scope of the conclusion: this is a property of OUR one-hot "packed8" ZoKrates encoding
(a tree decoder over 2,048 slots per row, times 7 rows). It is not a property of
zkStream-style operators, of Groth16, or of R1CS in general. A memory-checked R1CS
encoding would need an in-circuit Fiat-Shamir challenge, and was not built. The
external comparison therefore stays at d=3/w=1024. Report the pre-registered d=7
arm as this measured refusal.
